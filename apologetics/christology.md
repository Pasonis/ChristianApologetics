## Summary

The Orthodox confession is that the one Lord Jesus Christ is the eternal Son
and Word of God, one of the Holy Trinity, truly God and truly man, one Person
in two complete natures.

He is not a mere man elevated by God, not a divine being merely appearing as
man, not two sons joined by honor or relation, and not a blended third thing.
He is the Word of God incarnate.

## 1. One Person / Hypostasis

The one eternal Son of God became man, and therefore the same Christ acts and
is spoken of according to both His divinity and His humanity.

| Statement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **One and the same Son** | Christ is not two Sons, two Christs, or two personal subjects. |
| **Passible in flesh** | Christ truly suffers and dies according to His humanity. |
| **Impassible in Godhead** | The divine nature does not suffer, change, or die. |
| **Bounded in body** | His human body is truly local, visible, and touchable. |
| **Boundless in Godhead** | As God, He remains uncircumscribed and omnipresent. |
| **Finite and infinite** | Finite according to humanity; infinite according to divinity. |

This is the basis of the [**communication of idioms**](#glossary-comm-of-idioms): things proper to either
nature can be predicated of the **one Person**. We can say “God suffered in
the flesh,” not because the divine nature became passible, but because the one
who suffered according to the flesh is truly God the Word.

<details>
<summary>Dogmatic / conciliar sources</summary>

### Council of Ephesus, 431

[Ephesus][1] directly rejects the separation of Christ into two Sons. In
Cyril's letter to Nestorius, approved by the council, the union is described as
the Word `"personally united to himself flesh animated by a rational soul,"` so
that "`there is of both one Christ and one Son`". The council therefore rejects
any account in which Christ is divided into a divine Son and a human son:
`"We must not, therefore, divide the one Lord Jesus Christ into two Sons."`

Ephesus also rejects the idea that the Word merely united Himself to an
already-existing human person. Cyril says that Christ `"was not first born a
common man of the Holy Virgin, and then the Word came down and entered into
him."` Rather, `"the Scripture has not said that the Word united to himself the
person of man, but that he was made flesh."`

For this reason Ephesus protects the confession of **Theotokos**. Mary is
Mother of God `"not as if the nature of the Word or his divinity had its
beginning from the Holy Virgin,"` but because the body born from her was
personally united to the Word, who is therefore `"said to be born according to
the flesh."`

* The Epistle of Cyril to Nestorius("Intelligo quosdam meæ")

### Council of Chalcedon, 451

[Chalcedon][6] confesses Christ as `"one and the same [Person],"` `"perfect in
Godhead and perfect in manhood, very God and very man."` He is `"consubstantial
with the Father as touching his Godhead, and consubstantial with us as touching
his manhood."`

Chalcedon further teaches that this `"one and the same Jesus Christ"` is confessed
`"in two natures, unconfusedly, immutably, indivisibly, inseparably."` The union
does not remove `"the distinction of natures,"` but preserves `"the peculiar
property of each nature."` These two natures are `"united in one Person and
subsistence,"` `"not separated or divided into two persons,"` but are the one and
same `"Son and only-begotten, God the Word, our Lord Jesus Christ"`.

* Session V

Chalcedon guards both sides:

| Error rejected | Orthodox confession |
|---|---|
| **Confusion / mixture** | The natures are united "unconfusedly." This rejects Eutychian confusion of the natures. |
| **Change** | The natures are united "immutably." The Word does not change into flesh, and the humanity is not swallowed up into divinity. |
| [**Division**](#one-person-heresy-nestorianism) | The natures are united "indivisibly." Christ is not divided into a divine subject and a human subject. |
| [**Separation**](#heresy-prosopic-union) | The natures are united "inseparably." The union is not merely moral, external, relational, or prosopic. |
| [**Two-subject Christology**](#one-person-heresy-nestorianism) | Christ is "not separated or divided into two persons," but one and the same Son, God the Word. |
| **Eutychianism / confused-nature Christology** | Eutyches; condemned at Constantinople 448 and Chalcedon 451 | 5th century | Confuses or collapses Christ's humanity and divinity, often expressed as Christ being "from two natures" before the union but not continuing "in two natures" after the union |

Note: *"Separation" broadly includes earlier errors like Cerinthian and Gnostic
separationism, where the heavenly Christ is separable from the man Jesus.
However, in the Chalcedonian context, "inseparably" most directly guards
against Nestorian or prosopic accounts in which the union is treated as
external, moral, relational, or merely according to appearance.*

### Second Council of Constantinople, 553

[Constantinople II][3] clarifies Chalcedon against Nestorian misreadings. It
anathematizes anyone who says that `“the wonder-working Word of God is one”` and
`“the Christ that suffered another,”` or that the Word was merely “with” the man
born of woman.

It also rejects the idea that the union is merely by grace, dignity, honor,
authority, relation, or good pleasure. Instead, the union is **hypostatic**, so
that there is only one Person, our Lord Jesus Christ, one of the Holy Trinity.

Constantinople II says the union is not:

> God favoring, indwelling, honoring, or empowering a separate human person.

It is:

> God the Word Himself becoming man.

* The Capitula of the Council 3

### Third Council of Constantinople, 680–681

[Constantinople III][4] confesses two natural wills and two natural operations in
`“our one Lord Jesus Christ,”` not as separated into two persons or subsistences.
This matters because Orthodox Christology does not preserve Christ's unity by
erasing His humanity.

* The Letter of Pope Agatho

### Nestorianism
<a id="one-person-heresy-nestorianism"></a>

| Orthodox confession | Nestorian tendency |
|---|---|
| One Christ | Two subjects acting side by side |
| One Son | A divine Son and a human son |
| One Hypostasis of the Logos incarnate | A conjunction of the Logos with a man |
| Mary is **Theotokos** because the one born of her is God incarnate | Mary is only **Christotokos**, as though she bore merely the human Christ |

The problem is not merely that Nestorius crudely said “two Christs.” He often
denied that charge. Cyril's argument is more precise: Nestorius's way of
explaining the union entails two Sons even when it uses unity-language.

In his Second Letter to Nestorius, Cyril says that if the hypostatic union is
rejected, “we fall into the fallacy of speaking of two sons.” Even speaking of
a “union of persons” is not enough, because Scripture does not say that the
Word united the person of a man to Himself, but that “the Word became flesh.”

In *Against Nestorius*, Cyril says that Nestorius's party “sunder[s], dividing
into two several sons, the One Lord Jesus Christ,” because they make the Word
connected to a man by dignity, honor, or external relation, rather than
confessing that the Word Himself became man..

The entailments of Nestorius' actual positions are illustrated in [The Twelve Anathemas of St. Cyril Against Nestorius](https://www.newadvent.org/fathers/3810.htm).

#### Prosopic union
<a id="one-person-heresy-prosopic-union"></a>

A **prosopic union** is a union according to appearance, face, role, dignity,
honor, name, worship, relation, or manifestation rather than a true
hypostatic union.

This is rejected because it leaves Christ as effectively two subjects appearing
as one, instead of the one divine Hypostasis of the Word incarnate.

| Orthodox confession | Prosopic / Nestorianizing tendency |
|---|---|
| The union is hypostatic | The union is by appearance, honor, or shared role |
| One Person acts according to two natures | Two subjects cooperate under one name |
| The Word Himself is born, suffers, and dies in the flesh | The man Jesus is born and suffers while the Word is externally joined |

### Adoptionism / Dynamic Monarchianism
<a id="one-person-heresy-adoptionism"></a>

**Core error:** Jesus is a man who is later adopted, empowered, indwelt, or
specially inspired by God, rather than the eternal Son and Word of God Himself
becoming man.

**Main figures:** Theodotus of Byzantium, Artemon, Paul of Samosata, and later
Photinus of Sirmium.

**Core critique:** God the Son Himself became man. God did not merely choose,
adopt, inspire, empower, or indwell a righteous man.

| Orthodox confession | Adoptionist / Dynamic Monarchian tendency |
|---|---|
| The Person of Christ is the pre-eternal Son and Word | The person of Jesus is a human man specially graced by God |
| The Word became flesh | The Word, Spirit, or divine power comes upon or dwells in Jesus |
| Mary bore the Word incarnate according to the flesh | Mary bore a man uniquely indwelt or empowered by God |
| Christ is Son by nature | Jesus receives divine sonship by adoption, grace, empowerment, or moral excellence |
| Christ's humanity belongs personally to the Word | The humanity belongs to a human subject elevated by God |

Hippolytus reports that Theodotus taught Christ was "a man of a kindred nature
with all men" and that "Christ descended upon Jesus at His baptism." Eusebius
similarly describes the Theodotian/Artemonite error as teaching that the
Saviour was "a mere man."

Paul of Samosata represents a more sophisticated form of the same tendency. The
Synod of Antioch accused him of refusing to confess that "the Son of God came
down from heaven" and of saying that "Jesus Christ is from below." In this
model, the Logos is related to or dwells in the man Jesus, rather than the
Logos Himself becoming man.

Photinus of Sirmium later continues this basic pattern by denying the
pre-existence of the Son and treating Christ as beginning from Mary, with divine
sonship grounded in indwelling or empowerment rather than in the eternal Word's
incarnation.

### Ebionism / mere-man Christology
<a id="heresy-ebionism"></a>

**Core error:** Jesus is treated as a righteous man, prophet, or Messiah, but
not the eternal Son and Logos incarnate.

**Main figures / groups:** The Ebionites. There is no universally accepted
single founder named “Ebion”; many scholars treat “Ebionites” as a label
applied to certain Jewish-Christian groups.

**Why it violates one-person Christology:** Orthodoxy says the Person of Christ
is the pre-eternal Son. Ebionism effectively says the person of Jesus is a
human person specially favored by God.

### Cerinthian separationism
<a id="heresy-cerinthian-separationism"></a>

**Core error:** Cerinthus distinguished between Jesus and the Christ. In this
model, Jesus is a mere man, and “the Christ” descends upon Him at baptism, then
departs before the crucifixion.

| Orthodox | Cerinthian tendency |
|---|---|
| Jesus Christ is one Person | Jesus and “the Christ” are functionally separable |
| The Son Himself suffers in the flesh | The heavenly Christ departs before suffering |
| The Incarnation is real | The union is temporary and external |

### Gnostic separationism

Some Gnostic systems treat the heavenly Christ as a divine revealer who only
passes through, uses, or appears through the man Jesus. This is separationist
for the same basic reason: Jesus and the divine Christ are not confessed as one
and the same incarnate Person.

<summary>2. Two Natures: true God and true man without confusion or separation</summary>

## 2. Two Natures

Christ is one Person in two complete and distinct natures: divine and human.
The natures are not divided into two Christs, but neither are they blended into
a third thing.

> Two natures there are, God and man (since there are both soul and body), but
> not two “sons” or two “Gods”; though Paul spoke of the “inner” and “outer”
> man, we are not dealing with two human beings.

*St Gregory of Nazianzus, On God and Christ: The Five Theological Orations and
Two Letters to Cledonius, Popular Patristics Series Book 23, p. 160.*

The Chalcedonian boundaries are:

| Boundary | Error rejected |
|---|---|
| **Without confusion** | No blending into a third thing |
| **Without change** | Divinity does not become humanity; humanity does not become divinity by nature |
| **Without division** | The two natures are not divided into two Christs |
| **Without separation** | The union is never merely external or temporary |

<details>
<summary>Dogmatic / conciliar sources</summary>

### Council of Chalcedon, 451

Chalcedon gives the classic two-natures formula:

> one and the same Christ, Son, Lord, Only-begotten, recognized in two natures,
> without confusion, without change, without division, without separation.

The phrase “in two natures” must be read together with “one and the same Son.”
The natures are distinguished, but the subject is not divided.

### Second Council of Constantinople, 553

Constantinople II clarifies that “in two natures” must not be used to divide
Christ into two subjects. The distinction of natures remains, but the union is
hypostatic.

### Third Council of Constantinople, 680–681

Constantinople III teaches that because Christ has two complete natures, He has
two natural wills and two natural operations, divine and human. This does not
create two persons, because will and operation belong to nature, while the
acting subject is the one Christ.

### Eutychianism / extreme Monophysitism
<a id="heresy-eutychianism"></a>

**Core error:** After the union, Christ's humanity is swallowed up, absorbed,
confused, or reduced by the divinity.

| Orthodox | Eutychian tendency |
|---|---|
| One Person in two natures | One Person with humanity absorbed into divinity |
| Full divinity and full humanity remain | Humanity is diminished or overwhelmed |
| No division, no confusion | Avoids division by falling into confusion |

Eutychianism is the opposite-side error from Nestorianism:

| Nestorianism | Eutychianism |
|---|---|
| Divides the person / subject | Confuses or absorbs the natures |
| Two-subject tendency | One-nature collapse |
| Protects distinction wrongly | Protects unity wrongly |

### Monophysitism and the Oriental Orthodox question
<a id="heresy-monophysitism-oo"></a>

Eastern Orthodoxy usually distinguishes **Eutychian Monophysitism** from the
self-understanding of the **Oriental Orthodox**, who usually prefer
**Miaphysite** language: “one incarnate nature of God the Word,” from St Cyril
of Alexandria.

The Chalcedonian critique is not simply:

> Anyone who says “one nature” must mean Eutyches.

The better critique is:

> Any formula must be judged by whether it preserves full divinity, full
> humanity, no confusion, no change, no division, no separation, and one divine
> subject.

#### Oriental Orthodox / anti-Chalcedonian excerpt

If someone says:

> “We reject Chalcedon because people could take ‘two natures’ in a Nestorian
> way,”

then the Chalcedonian reply is:

> “People could also take ‘one nature’ in a Eutychian or tertium quid way. The
> question is not what can be misread, but what the formula actually teaches
> when interpreted by its own definitions.”

Chalcedon defines itself with the guardrails:

> without confusion, without change, without division, without separation.

So a Nestorian reading is not merely “one possible interpretation” of
Chalcedon; it is explicitly excluded by Chalcedon.

Likewise, if the Oriental Orthodox say “one incarnate nature” while also
confessing full divinity, full humanity, no confusion, no change, no tertium
quid, and one divine subject, then the Chalcedonian can say:

> “That sounds materially orthodox. But then why reject the council that says
> the same thing with different guardrails?”

The critique is:

> If Cyril's anti-division language gets to be interpreted according to its
> orthodox intention, then Chalcedon's anti-division language should also be
> interpreted according to its own orthodox intention.

### Nestorian division of the natures

This is the opposite error from Eutychianism. Instead of confusing the natures,
it separates them into two subjects. Two natures must never be read as two
personal centers.

| Divine nature | Human nature |
|---|---|
| uncreated | created |
| impassible according to divinity | passible according to humanity |
| eternal | born in time |
| omnipotent | capable of hunger, fatigue, suffering, death |

But both columns belong to **one and the same Son**.

### Monothelitism and Monoenergism

Monothelitism and Monoenergism belong here because two complete natures imply
two natural wills and two natural operations. These wills and operations are
not two personal subjects; they are the natural capacities of the one Christ.

</details>

</details>

<summary>3. Fully Divine: consubstantial with the Father</summary>

## 3. Fully Divine

The one Person who became man is not a creature, angel, demigod, inspired
prophet, or adopted son. He is the eternal Son of God, true God of true God,
of one essence with the Father.

<details>
<summary>Dogmatic / conciliar sources</summary>

### Nicene-Constantinopolitan Creed

> Light of Light. True God of True God. Begotten, not created, of one essence
> with the Father; through whom all things were made.

### Council of Chalcedon, 451

Chalcedon confesses Christ as:

> complete in Godhead... truly God.

### Second Council of Constantinople, 553

Constantinople II confesses that the crucified Christ is:

> true God, and the Lord of Glory, and one of the Holy Trinity.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Biblical sources</summary>

### John 1:1–3

The Word was God, and all things were made through Him.

### John 8:58

> “Before Abraham was, I AM.”

### John 10:30

> “I and the Father are one.”

### John 20:28

> “My Lord and my God.”

### Colossians 1:15–20

All things were created through Christ and for Christ.

### Colossians 2:9

> “For in Him dwells all the fullness of the Godhead bodily.”

### Hebrews 1

The Son is the radiance of the Father's glory, worshiped by angels, and
addressed as God.

### Titus 2:13 and 2 Peter 1:1

Christ is confessed as God and Savior.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Church Fathers</summary>

### St Athanasius

Athanasius' anti-Arian theology belongs here: only if the Son is truly God can
He save, deify, and unite us to God. A creature cannot bridge the gulf between
God and creation by nature.

### St Gregory of Nazianzus

Gregory's confession of “one and the same God and Son” also belongs here,
because the subject who becomes man is already God and Son before the ages.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Errors and heresies related to Christ's full divinity</summary>

| Heresy / error | Main figures / groups | Approx. origin | Main issue |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Arianism** | Arius; Arian parties | early 4th century; condemned at Nicaea 325 | The Son is a creature, not true God |
| **Semi-Arianism / Homoiousianism** | Various 4th-century anti-Nicene groups | 4th century | The Son is like the Father, but not consubstantial |
| **Eunomianism / Anomoeanism** | Aetius; Eunomius | 4th century | The Son is unlike the Father in essence |
| **Ebionism** | Ebionites | 1st–2nd century | Jesus is not the pre-existent divine Son |
| **Adoptionism** | Theodotus; Paul of Samosata; Photinus | 2nd–4th century | Jesus becomes Son by grace or adoption, not eternal Son by nature |
| **Socinianism / Unitarian Christology** | Faustus Socinus; later Unitarian movements | 16th century onward | Denies Christ's true deity and eternal Sonship |

### Arianism

Arianism teaches that the Son is exalted above other creatures but is still a
creature: “there was when He was not.” This destroys the Nicene confession that
Christ is true God, begotten not made, of one essence with the Father.

### Semi-Arianism / Homoiousianism

Semi-Arian or Homoiousian formulas say the Son is “like” the Father in essence,
but avoid or reject “of one essence.” Orthodoxy rejects this because likeness is
not enough: the Son is consubstantial with the Father.

### Eunomianism / Anomoeanism

Eunomianism goes further and says the Son is unlike the Father in essence. This
is a radical denial of Nicene Christology.

<summary>4. Fully Human: complete manhood assumed and healed</summary>

## 4. Fully Human

The Son assumed complete human nature: body, rational soul/mind, human will,
human operation, passibility, mortality, and everything proper to humanity
except sin.

The classical patristic rule is:

> That which He has not assumed He has not healed.

If Christ did not assume a human mind, the human mind is not healed. If Christ
did not assume a human will, the human will is not healed. If Christ did not
assume mortal flesh, mortal flesh is not healed.

<details>
<summary>Dogmatic / conciliar sources</summary>

### Council of Constantinople I, 381

Constantinople I rejects Apollinarianism, which mutilated Christ's humanity by
denying Him a complete human rational soul/mind.

### Council of Chalcedon, 451

Chalcedon confesses Christ as:

> complete in manhood... truly man... of a reasonable soul and body.

### Third Council of Constantinople, 680–681

Constantinople III confesses two natural wills and two natural operations in
Christ. This protects the fullness of Christ's humanity: He has a real human
will and real human operation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Biblical sources</summary>

### Luke 2:52

Christ grows in wisdom and stature.

### John 1:14

The Word became flesh.

### Matthew 4:2

Christ hungers.

### John 4:6

Christ becomes weary.

### John 11:35

Christ weeps.

### Hebrews 2:14–17

He partook of flesh and blood and was made like His brethren.

### Hebrews 4:15

He was tempted in all things as we are, yet without sin.

### Matthew 26:39 / Luke 22:42

> “Not My will, but Yours, be done.”

This reveals real human willing in Christ, perfectly obedient to the Father.

### Luke 23:46

Christ truly dies.

### Luke 24:39

The risen Christ has real flesh and bones.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Church Fathers</summary>

### St Gregory of Nazianzus

> That which He has not assumed He has not healed.

Gregory's rule is especially important against Apollinarianism. Christ must
have a complete human mind/soul because the human mind/soul also needs healing.

### St Maximus the Confessor

Maximus is central against Monothelitism. Christ has a true human will, but it
is never rebellious or opposed to God. It is the human will of the one incarnate
Word, freely and perfectly obedient.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Errors and heresies related to Christ's full humanity</summary>

| Heresy / error | Main figures / groups | Approx. origin | Main issue |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| **Docetism** | Various Gnostic/docetic groups | late 1st–2nd century | Christ only seemed human or only seemed to suffer |
| **Apollinarianism** | Apollinarius of Laodicea | mid-late 4th century; condemned 381 | The Logos replaces the rational human soul/mind in Christ |
| **Monothelitism** | Sergius; Cyrus; Honorius; imperial compromise theology | 7th century; condemned 680–681 | Christ has only one will, undermining full humanity |
| **Monoenergism** | 7th-century compromise theology | 7th century | Christ has only one operation/energy |
| **Eutychianism** | Eutyches | mid-5th century | Christ's humanity is absorbed or reduced |
| **Aphthartodocetism** | Julian of Halicarnassus and related parties | 6th century | Christ's body is treated as incorruptible in a way that can undermine real passibility |

### Docetism
<a id="heresy-docetism"></a>

**Core error:** Christ only appeared to have a real body, suffer, die, or rise
bodily.

Docetism does not usually split Christ into two persons like Nestorianism does.
Instead, it attacks the Incarnation from the other side: the divine Person does
not truly assume our full humanity.

Orthodoxy says:

> The one Person of the Son truly became man, truly suffered in the flesh,
> truly died, and truly rose bodily.

### Apollinarianism
<a id="heresy-apollinarianism"></a>

**Core error:** Christ has a human body and perhaps a lower soul, but not a
complete rational human mind/soul. The Logos takes the place of the rational
human principle.

Apollinarianism does not usually violate “one Person” by splitting Christ into
two subjects. In fact, Apollinarius was trying to protect Christ's unity. But
he did so by mutilating Christ's humanity.

| Orthodox | Apollinarian tendency |
|---|---|
| One divine Person assumes complete humanity | One divine Person assumes incomplete humanity |
| Christ has a real human mind/soul | The Logos replaces the human rational soul |
| Full humanity healed | Humanity only partially assumed |

### Monothelitism
<a id="heresy-monothelitism"></a>

**Core error:** Christ has only one will.

**Main figures:** Sergius of Constantinople, Cyrus of Alexandria, Emperor
Heraclius, Honorius of Rome. Orthodox opponents include St Sophronius of
Jerusalem and St Maximus the Confessor.

Monothelitism does not say there are two persons. But it undermines the
fullness of Christ's humanity. If Christ has no human will, then He has not
fully assumed human nature.

| Orthodox | Monothelite error |
|---|---|
| One Person, two natures, two natural wills | One Person, two natures, one will |
| Human will healed by obedience | Human will effectively absent |
| “Not My will, but Yours” reveals real human willing | Christ's human obedience becomes hard to explain |

### Monoenergism

**Core error:** Christ has only one operation or energy. This was a compromise
position related to the Monothelite controversy. Orthodoxy confesses two
natural operations because Christ has two complete natures.

### Aphthartodocetism

**Core error:** Christ's body is treated as incorruptible or impassible in a way
that can undermine His real passibility, suffering, and death. This is an
advanced category and can be omitted if the document needs to stay simpler.

</details>

## 6. Glossary

| Term | Short definition |
|---|---|
| **Hypostasis** | Concrete subsistence or personal subject; in Christology, the one Hypostasis of the Word. |
| **Nature / physis** | What something is; the essence with its natural properties; Christ has divine and human nature. |
| **Person / prosopon** | Person, face, or personal manifestation; orthodoxly, the one personal subject of Christ, but the term can be ambiguous depending on context. |
| **Subsistence** | The concrete existing of a nature; sometimes used for hypostasis, sometimes for the way a nature exists in a hypostasis. |
| **Idiom / idiomata** | Proper characteristics or distinguishing properties; can refer to natural properties or hypostatic/personal marks depending on context. |
| **Prosopic union** | Union according to appearance, dignity, honor, name, relation, or manifestation, not true hypostatic union. |
| **Hypostatic union** | The union of divine and human natures in the one Hypostasis of the Word. |
| **Anhypostatic** | Christ's humanity has no separate human hypostasis of its own; it is not an independently existing human person. |
| **Enhypostatic** | Christ's humanity has real concrete existence in the Hypostasis of the Word. |
| <a id="glossary-comm-of-idioms">**Communication of idioms**</a> | Because the Person is one, things proper to either nature can be predicated of the one Christ. |
| **Theotokos** | Mary is Mother of God because the one born from her is God the Word incarnate. |
| **Dyophysite** | Confessing Christ in two natures. |
| **Miaphysite** | Confessing “one incarnate nature” of God the Word, usually in Oriental Orthodox usage. |
| **Monophysite** | Literally “one-nature”; in EO polemics often used for anti-Chalcedonian or Eutychian tendencies, though the term must be used carefully. |
| **Dyothelite** | Confessing Christ with two natural wills. |
| **Dyoenergist** | Confessing Christ with two natural operations/energies. |
