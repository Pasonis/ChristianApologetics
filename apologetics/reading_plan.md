### 1. **OCA Lives of the Saints — free online**

The Orthodox Church in America has a daily **Lives of the Saints** page with saints and feasts for each day of the liturgical year, often with icons, troparia, and kontakia. ([Orthodox Church in America][1])

### 2. **The Prologue of Ohrid — St. Nikolai Velimirovich**

This is one of the classic Orthodox daily saint-reading books. It usually includes:

* lives of saints for the day
* short reflections
* homilies
* sometimes hymns or contemplations

It is more devotional and punchy than encyclopedic. Good for forming an Orthodox imagination, but sometimes the tone is intense in a very old-world hagiographical way. There are free online versions floating around; the Prologue has been available online for years, and there is even an open-source project based on it. ([GitHub][2])

### 3. **A Daily Calendar of Saints: A Synaxarion for Today’s North American Church — Fr. Lawrence Farley**

This is a more accessible modern option. Ancient Faith lists it as a daily calendar of saints with one or more saints for each day of the year. ([Ancient Faith Store][3])

This may be less intimidating than the full Synaxarion and more manageable for daily reading.

### Good specific saint lives to read early

**St. Anthony the Great** — classic desert monasticism. [4][4] [5][5] [6][6]<br>
**St. Mary of Egypt** — repentance, ascetic struggle, mercy.<br>
**St. Moses the Black** — radical repentance and humility.<br>
**St. Seraphim of Sarov** — prayer, joy, Holy Spirit.[8][8]<br>
**St. Herman of Alaska** — missionary humility and North American Orthodoxy.[9][9] [10][10] [11][11]<br>
**St. Silouan the Athonite** — modern Orthodox spirituality, humility, prayer for the whole world.[7][7]<br>
**St. Maria of Paris** — modern sanctity expressed through sacrifice and service.<br>
**St. Nektarios of Aegina** — patience under slander and suffering.
**St. Paisios the Athonite** — modern elder, pastoral discernment, prayer.[12][12] [13][13]<br>

For your current interests, I would especially put **St. Anthony, St. Mary of Egypt, St. Maximus, St. Gregory Palamas, St. Seraphim, St. Silouan, and St. Paisios** on the radar, but not all at once.
