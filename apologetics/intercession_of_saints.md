# Sola Scriptura Case to Protestants

Note: the Protestant canon may not give a woodenly exact scene of: `"A Christian on earth asks departed Saint X in heaven to pray for him."`
However, it does provide the biblical categories necessary for the practice:

* intercession
* the power of righteous prayer
* asking righteous servants of God to pray for us
* the continued life of the departed righteous
* heavenly participation in prayer before God

Taken cumulatively, this forms a biblical framework in which the intercession of saints is not foreign to Scripture, even if an exact liturgical formula is not explicitly written.

* [1 Timothy 2:1](#1-timothy-21) commands supplications, prayers, intercessions, and thanksgivings for all people.
* [James 5:16](#james-516) says the prayer of a righteous person is powerful.
* [1 Samuel 12:19,23](#1-samuel-1219-23) shows the people explicitly asking Samuel, a righteous servant of God, to pray for them, and Samuel treats interceding for them as part of his duty before God.

<details>
<summary>Objections</summary>

## "But Samuel was alive on earth."

The question then becomes: `Does death sever the righteous from life in God?` Scripture says no:

* [Matthew 22:31–32](#matthew-2231-32) — God is not God of the dead but of the living.
* [Hebrews 12:1](#hebrews-121) — we are surrounded by a great cloud of witnesses.

Scripture also depicts heavenly participation in prayer:

* [Revelation 5:8](#revelation-58)
* [Revelation 8:3–4](#revelation-834)

These show heavenly beings participating in the presentation of prayers before God.

</details>

## Final point

The Protestant objection generally survives only by demanding an exact proof-text for the full liturgical formula: `"Christian on earth asks departed Saint X in heaven to pray."` But Protestants themselves frequently accept doctrines and practices through cumulative biblical reasoning rather than a single explicit verse.

<details>
<summary>Accepted cumulative Examples</summary>

* Trinity - No verse says: `"God is one essence in three co-equal persons."` The doctrine is derived from the totality of Scripture.

* Sunday worship replacing Sabbath worship - No verse says: `"Christians shall now gather every Sunday instead of Saturday."` The practice develops from apostolic example and cumulative scriptural evidence.

* The canon of Scripture - No inspired table of contents exists in Scripture. Scripture never says: `"The New Testament consists of these exact 27 books."``

* Two natures of Christ - No verse says: `"Jesus Christ is one divine Person possessing two complete natures."` This is theological synthesis from Scripture.

* The word "omniscient" - No verse explicitly says: `"God is omniscient."` The concept is derived from multiple passages.

* Youth groups, altar calls, sinner's prayer

</details>

These are common Protestant practices, but no verse commands them directly.

The question therefore becomes: `Must every Christian doctrine or practice have an explicit proof-text, or may truths be recognized through the cumulative witness of Scripture?`
