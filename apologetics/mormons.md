# Creed Violations

<summary>Not one God in the Nicene sense</summary>

## Not one God in the Nicene sense

Creed: "...of one essence with the Father..."

LDS believe the Father, Son, and Spirit are separate divine beings/personages, not one essence.

P1: Historic Christianity is defined dogmatically by the Nicene confession of one God in one divine essence: Father, Son, and Holy Spirit.

P2: LDS doctrine denies this by teaching that the Father, Son, and Holy Spirit are three separate divine beings/personages rather than one God in one essence.

C: Therefore, LDS doctrine is outside historic Nicene Christianity.

<details>
<summary>Evidence from Mormon sources</summary>

Official LDS “Gospel Topics” says:

> “The members of the Godhead are three separate beings. The Father and the Son have tangible bodies of flesh and bones, and the Holy Ghost is a personage of spirit.” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][1])

The LDS “Godhead” study guide likewise says:

> “The Godhead consists of three divine beings: Heavenly Father, Jesus Christ, and the Holy Ghost. Though separate persons, They work together in perfect unity…” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][2])

Their missionary-facing page explicitly contrasts this with the traditional Trinity:

> “We don’t believe in the traditional concept of the Trinity. We believe that the Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost are three separate beings who are one in purpose... We believe They are three distinct personages, not one singular being. We call Them the Godhead.” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][3])

Jeffrey R. Holland stated in General Conference:

> “The Father, the Son, and the Holy Ghost are separate persons, three divine beings.” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][5])

### Why this is not Nicene

The Nicene Creed confesses the Son as “consubstantial with the Father,” meaning *of one substance/essence with the Father*. Britannica summarizes the Nicene formula as saying the Son is *homoousios* with the Father, “of one substance with the Father,” and therefore fully what the Father is. ([Encyclopedia Britannica][6])

| Nicene Christianity                                               | LDS doctrine                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| One God in one divine essence/substance                           | Godhead of three divine beings/personages              |
| Father, Son, and Spirit are distinct persons, not separate beings | Father, Son, and Spirit are separate beings/personages |
| Unity is ontological: one essence                                 | Unity is functional/moral: one in purpose              |

<summary>Father is not the Maker</summary>

Creed: "...the Father, the Maker of heaven and Earth and of all things visible and invisible... through whom all things were made..."

LDS doctrine teaches that Jesus Christ shaped or organized the heavens and earth from elements that already existed.

P1: Nicene Christianity confesses one God, the Father Almighty, maker of heaven and earth and of all things visible and invisible, with all things made through the Son. This entails that all created reality depends on God for its very existence, not merely for its organization.

P2: LDS doctrine denies this Nicene doctrine of creation by teaching that matter/elements and intelligence are uncreated or eternal, and that creation is the organization of preexisting materials rather than God bringing all things from non-being into being.

C: Therefore, LDS doctrine is outside historic Nicene Christianity on the doctrine of God as Creator, because it denies that the Father, through the Son, is the absolute source of all things visible and invisible.

Yes, the **core claim is valid**, but I would **not** frame it as “the Father worked with Heavenly Mother” unless you have a very specific LDS source saying that. The stronger, cleaner argument is:

1. **Nicene Christianity** confesses the Father as the one God and “maker of heaven and earth, of all things visible and invisible,” and the Son as the one “through whom all things were made.” ([USCCB][1])
2. **Orthodox/Nicene Christianity** understands this as creation **out of nothing**, meaning God brings into being what did not previously exist; He is not merely arranging eternal stuff. OCA’s Orthodox Faith explicitly says “to create means to make out of nothing” and to bring “from non-existence into being.” ([Orthodox Church in America][2])
3. **LDS doctrine denies creation out of nothing**. The official LDS Gospel Topics entry says Jesus Christ created the heavens and earth “organizing elements that already existed” and “did not create the world ‘out of nothing.’” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][3])
4. LDS scripture/doctrine also teaches that “intelligence… was not created or made, neither indeed can be,” and Joseph Smith taught that “element had an existence from the time He had” and “had no beginning and can have no end.” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][4])


<details>
<summary>Evidence from Mormon sources</summary>

</details>
Supporting LDS evidence: Abraham 4 says “the Gods organized and formed the heavens and the earth,” and LDS teaching manuals summarize this as “the earth and the heavens were made from existing materials” and “the Gods worked together to organize and form the heavens and the earth.” ([The Church of Jesus Christ][5])

For the **monarchia** angle, I’d phrase it this way:

> In Nicene/Orthodox theology, the Father is the personal source within the Trinity and, as the one God with His Son and Spirit, the Creator of all that exists. LDS theology weakens or denies this in two ways: first, by making matter/intelligence co-eternal and uncreated; second, by presenting creation as the cooperative organization of preexistent materials by plural divine beings. Therefore, the LDS Father is not the Nicene Father as absolute uncreated source of all created reality.

That is much harder for an LDS interlocutor to dodge than “the Father didn’t work alone,” because Nicene Christianity also does **not** say the Father creates “alone” in isolation from the Son and Spirit. The real contradiction is **creation ex nihilo vs. organization of eternal matter/intelligence**.
