# outline

1. If the divine essence lacks knowing, prescience, creating, providence, and deification, then it is not truly God. ([Agape Biblia][1])
2. If it has them but only acquired them later, then there was a time when God was not fully God. ([Agape Biblia][1])
3. Therefore, if God eternally possesses these, then these powers are also unoriginate and uncreated. But they are **not separate essences**; they are powers/energies that “inhere in” the one divine essence. They are really distinct but no fully distinct.([Agape Biblia][1])

**Palamas is not splitting God into essence plus extra divine things. He is denying that God’s eternal life and operations can be reduced to the essence alone, because then God would either lack them or acquire them, or treated as created effects.**

# simplified process

grace → illumination → wonder → awakened desire for God → repentance, prayer, and pursuit of God → purification → receptivity to deeper deifying grace

Or more plainly:

God first illumines a person, this awakens wonder and desire for Him, and that desire must be acted upon in repentance, prayer, and purification, which then makes the person receptive to deeper grace.

# definitions

**Knowing**
This means God’s eternal knowledge: God is not blank or inert in Himself. “Knowing” here is not discursive reasoning, learning, or moving from ignorance to information. It is God’s proper divine cognition, including self-knowledge and knowledge of all things. Palamas’s point is that if God truly is God, this knowing cannot be created or newly acquired. He later ties this to divine knowledge existing “before choosing.” ([Agape Biblia][1])

**Prescience**
Prescience is foreknowledge, but in Palamas it is not mere prediction. It is God’s eternal knowing of creatures and events prior to and apart from temporal succession. He explicitly asks, “how does it follow that the divine prescience had a beginning?” For him, to say God’s prescience began would imply a change from not-knowing to knowing. ([Agape Biblia][1])

**Creating**
This is God’s creative power: the power by which creatures come to be from nonbeing. Palamas is not saying creation itself is eternal in the same way creatures are eternal. He is saying the **divine power to create** is uncreated and without beginning, even though creatures begin in time. That is part of why he distinguishes divine powers from created effects. ([Agape Biblia][1])

**Embracing all things in itself**
This means something like God’s all-containing, all-comprehending mode of knowing and holding all things within His causal and intelligible grasp. It is not that creatures are literally parts of the divine essence. Rather, all things are contained in God as known, sustained, and encompassed by Him. In context, it is one more way of naming divine all-sufficiency and transcendence over all things. ([Agape Biblia][1])

**Providence**
Providence is God’s ordering, governing, and sustaining care for creation. Palamas immediately expands on this and says providence had to exist before creation in order for created things to come to be in time. He even says there is “a single unoriginate providence, that of God, and it is a work of God,” while providences other than it are created. So providence here is not just “planning,” but God’s eternal providential activity. ([Agape Biblia][1])

**The power of deification**
This is the divine power by which creatures, especially humans, are united to God by grace and made “partakers of the divine nature” without becoming the divine essence. In Palamite theology, deifying grace is not the essence but a divine energy. Lossky’s summary of Palamas is direct: “Grace or deifying illumination is not the essence, but rather divine energy.” ([Agape Biblia][2])

**Really distinct**
Means the distinction is not just in our minds, not just verbal, and not just a change in perspective. Not identical, objectively different, but may still be inseparable or belong to one reality.

So if essence and energies are really distinct, that means:
	* the distinction corresponds to reality,
	* it is not a mere conceptual trick,
	* essence is not simply another word for energy.

**Fully distinct**
Independently existing, separable, self-subsisting, basically two or more different things side by side.

**Powers**
Here “powers” means what Palamas elsewhere also calls God’s **natural powers**, **operations**, or **energies**: God’s knowing, willing, creating, providence, deifying grace, and so on. They are “of” the divine nature, not creatures and not later add-ons. A helpful modern Orthodox summary puts it this way: the divine energies in God are “powers and natural attributes,” and God does not begin to be Creator or merciful only when creatures appear. ([Pappas Patristic Institute][2])

**Reality**
In this passage, “reality” is being used broadly for what is **really the case / what really exists / what has ontological standing**. Palamas is arguing against the formula “only the essence is unoriginate reality; everything else is created reality.” He denies that because God’s natural powers are also without beginning. But he does **not** use “reality” here to mean “separate substance” or “separate self-subsisting being.” That is why he immediately adds that the powers are not essences. ([agape-biblia.org][1])

* **“superessentiality…”** = apophatic ((of knowledge of God) obtained through negating concepts that might be applied to him. The opposite of cataphatic) apprehension of God beyond all created concepts. ([Agape Biblia][3])
* **“superessential essence”** = the divine essence, not the energies. ([Agape Biblia][3])

# common errors 

That is probably the most important phrase here:

**really distinct, but inseparable**

## Error 1: Collapse everything into the essence

That would mean:

* grace is really essence
* deification is participation in the essence
* manifestation of God is just essence showing up directly

EO rejects that.

## Error 2: Make the energies into separate beings or parts

That would mean:

* multiple divine things alongside God
* a composite God made of essence + energies as pieces

EO rejects that too.

So the Palamite position is:

* essence and energies are **distinct**
* but the energies are **the energies of the essence/nature**
* they are not creatures
* they are not extra hypostases
* they are not detachable components

# Very simple formulation

* **Essence**: what God is in Himself; inaccessible, incommunicable
* **Energies**: God’s uncreated life, power, operation, grace, light, manifestation; accessible and participable. His activity, in the sense of actuality or realized potential.

So when someone participates in God, EO says:

* not in the **essence**
* but truly in the **energies**

# 4 point framework

* **energy itself** = uncreated divine operation
* **created effect** = what happens in creatures
* **manifestation** = divine self-disclosure in an accommodated form
* **creaturely experience** = how the creature receives it

## 1. Energy itself

This means the **uncreated divine operation** itself, not a creaturely result.

Examples:

* God’s **knowing**
* God’s **will**
* God’s **life**
* God’s **creative power**
* God’s **providence**
* God’s **grace**
* God’s **deifying energy**
* the **divine light** in Palamite language

These are not creatures, not effects in us, and not separate beings. They are God as active, manifest, and communicable.

---

## 2. Created effect

This means what happens **in creation** because of the energy.

Examples:

* the universe coming into being
* a plant growing by divine providence
* a human mind being illuminated
* a sinner repenting
* a saint becoming holier
* bodily healing
* bread and wine being consecrated as sacramental change in created reality
* a person’s virtues increasing

So:

* **creative energy** is uncreated
* **the world created by it** is a created effect
* **deifying grace/energy** is uncreated
* **my transformation in time** is a created effect in me

---

## 3. Manifestation

This is where God is **shown, disclosed, or encountered**.

It can be tricky because “manifestation” can refer either to the divine self-disclosure itself or to the created mode in which that disclosure appears.

Examples:

* the **light of Tabor**
* the **burning bush**
* the **cloud**
* the **fire**
* the **dove at Christ’s baptism**
* the **wrestler with Jacob**, on the stronger theophany reading

In EO thought, a manifestation often involves:

* **uncreated divine presence/energy**
* through a **created mode or sensible appearance**

So if you ask, “What is the manifestation?” the answer may include both:

* the divine self-disclosure
* the visible/tactile mode of appearance

---

## 4. Creaturely experience

This is the way the creature **receives** or **undergoes** the encounter.

Examples:

* Moses seeing the bush
* Jacob feeling resistance in the wrestling
* the apostles seeing the light at Tabor
* a saint experiencing illumination in prayer
* a Christian receiving grace
* awe, fear, joy, repentance, purification
* bodily sensation, sight, hearing, touch associated with a theophany

This is neither the divine essence nor the energy as such. It is the creature’s lived participation/reception.

---

### Putting events into categories

#### Example: Tabor

**Energy itself**

* uncreated divine light/glory

**Created effect**

* the disciples are strengthened, illumined, overwhelmed
* their condition changes in time

**Manifestation**

* Christ shines with divine glory before them

**Creaturely experience**

* they see the light, fall down, are afraid, perceive glory according to their capacity

---

#### Example: creation

**Energy itself**

* God’s uncreated creative power

**Created effect**

* heaven, earth, creatures

**Manifestation**

* creation manifests divine wisdom/power

**Creaturely experience**

* humans perceive the created world and infer God through it

---

#### Example: deification

**Energy itself**

* uncreated deifying grace

**Created effect**

* the saint is sanctified and transformed

**Manifestation**

* holiness, divine light, spiritual power may be shown in or through the saint

**Creaturely experience**

* the saint experiences communion with God; others may perceive holiness
